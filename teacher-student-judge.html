Teacher-Student-Judge

Synthetic preference data for training tool-using agents.

Manuscript under review

The problem

Agents that call tools over many turns fail in ways single-turn data does not capture: they lose track of the user's goal, call the wrong tool, or stop early. Preference training can fix this, but collecting human preferences over long, multi-turn tool-use conversations is slow and expensive.

Method

Three kinds of model work together to produce preference data without human labeling:

  • Teacher (Kimi-k2) shows strong behavior on the task.
  • Simulator (Qwen3-Coder-480B) plays the user across multi-turn conversations.
  • Judge compares candidate trajectories and decides which is preferred.

The pipeline produced about 8,000 preference pairs, used to train the student with Direct Preference Optimization.

Training

Student modelQwen3-30B-A3B (Mixture-of-Experts, 30B total, 3B active)
ObjectiveDPO on ~8K synthetic pairs
Hardware8×H100
ShardingPyTorch FSDP, then DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 via Hugging Face Accelerate
MemoryFP16 mixed precision, gradient accumulation, gradient checkpointing
